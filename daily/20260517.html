📄 听力原文

Good morning, everyone. Today we're going to explore an exciting frontier in computing: the marriage of quantum computing and artificial intelligence. Recent research has demonstrated that combining these two technologies can dramatically improve our ability to predict complex, chaotic systems. Why does this matter? Because many of the most challenging problems we face — from climate modeling to drug discovery — involve systems that are inherently chaotic and difficult to forecast.

Traditional computers struggle with chaos because they process information in a linear, step-by-step fashion. Quantum computers, on the other hand, use qubits that can exist in multiple states simultaneously, allowing them to explore many possible outcomes at once. When you add AI into the mix, something remarkable happens: the quantum computer identifies hidden patterns in vast datasets that classical computers would simply miss, and the AI uses those patterns to make remarkably accurate predictions.

In one striking experiment, researchers used a hybrid quantum-AI system to predict the behavior of turbulent fluid flows — a classic example of a chaotic system that has puzzled scientists for centuries. The hybrid system outperformed classical methods by a significant margin, correctly forecasting flow patterns that traditional models got wrong.

However, we must be realistic about the challenges. Current quantum computers are still limited in the number of qubits they can maintain, and they require extremely cold temperatures to operate. Error rates remain a concern. Nevertheless, the progress has been remarkable, and many experts believe we are on the verge of a quantum-AI revolution that will transform fields as diverse as medicine, finance, and climate science.

So what does this mean for the average person? In the coming decade, we may see quantum-AI systems helping to design more effective drugs, create more efficient batteries, and provide more accurate weather forecasts. The technology is still in its infancy, but the direction is clear: the future belongs to systems that combine the best of quantum mechanics and machine learning.

📄 听力原文

Today I'd like to discuss an important but often overlooked issue in American higher education: the gap between rural and urban students in access to selective colleges. While nearly a quarter of the American population lives in rural areas, only about three percent of students at elite universities come from these communities. This disparity has motivated a significant new initiative called the STARS College Network, which stands for Small Town and Rural Students.

The program was launched with a substantial investment from a wealthy alumnus who himself grew up in a small town. The initial twenty million dollars has since grown to one hundred and seventy million in total funding. The network has expanded from sixteen member schools to thirty-two, including prestigious institutions such as Brown, Columbia, Dartmouth, MIT, Stanford, and Yale. Each member school has committed to actively recruiting at rural high schools, which have traditionally been ignored by university admissions officers.

The results have been promising. Last year, more than ninety thousand rural students applied to member institutions, representing a fifteen percent increase from the previous year. However, getting students to apply is only half the battle. The real challenge now is convincing these students to actually enroll and, ultimately, to graduate.

This is not as straightforward as it might seem. Research shows that while ninety percent of rural students graduate from high school — a rate higher than their urban and suburban peers — only slightly more than half go directly to college. Furthermore, rural Americans are less likely than city dwellers to believe that college provides significant benefits, and they are more concerned about the potential negative effects of higher education on their communities and values.

To address these concerns, colleges are trying new approaches. Some are hosting special visitation days where admitted rural students can experience campus life. Others are providing additional financial aid and mentoring programs. The goal is not just to recruit rural students, but to ensure they feel welcome and supported throughout their college journey. This represents a significant shift in how elite institutions think about diversity — moving beyond race and income to include geographic background as a crucial dimension of a diverse student body.

GOFLOW: AI Maps Ocean Currents from Space

📝 核心词汇
current /ˈkɜːrənt/ n. 洋流，水流；adj. 当前的
satellite /ˈsætəlaɪt/ n. 卫星
reveal /rɪˈviːl/ v. 揭示，展现
dynamic /daɪˈnæmɪk/ adj. 动态的，不断变化的
pattern /ˈpætərn/ n. 模式，图案
prediction /prɪˈdɪkʃən/ n. 预测，预报
algorithm /ˈælɡərɪðəm/ n. 算法
monitor /ˈmɒnɪtər/ v. 监测，监控

A groundbreaking new AI-driven method called GOFLOW is transforming how scientists study ocean currents. By analyzing weather satellite images, this innovative technology can create highly detailed maps of ocean movement, revealing fast-moving currents that were previously difficult to track.

Ocean currents play a crucial role in regulating the Earth's climate. They transport warm and cold water around the globe, influence weather patterns, and affect marine ecosystems. Understanding how these currents behave is essential for climate prediction, shipping navigation, and fisheries management. However, traditional methods of monitoring ocean currents have significant limitations. Ships and buoys can only collect data from specific points, while satellite sensors primarily measure surface temperature rather than the movement of water itself.

GOFLOW takes a different approach. The system uses artificial intelligence to analyze sequences of thermal infrared images captured by weather satellites. By tracking how temperature patterns shift over time, the AI can infer the direction and speed of ocean currents with remarkable accuracy. This technique, known as optical flow analysis, has been used in computer vision for decades, but applying it to satellite imagery at a global scale required significant advances in machine learning.

The name GOFLOW stands for Global Ocean Flow. Developed by a team of researchers at several leading oceanographic institutions, the system processes terabytes of satellite data each day. The results are stunning: GOFLOW can detect currents as narrow as a few kilometers across, revealing intricate patterns of ocean circulation that were previously invisible.

One of the most exciting applications of GOFLOW is in hurricane forecasting. Ocean currents have a major influence on the intensity of tropical storms. Warm water fuels hurricanes, while cooler water can weaken them. By providing real-time data on ocean heat distribution and water movement, GOFLOW could help meteorologists predict storm intensity with greater precision.

Beyond weather forecasting, GOFLOW has implications for understanding climate change. As global temperatures rise, ocean currents may shift in ways that are difficult to predict with existing models. Continuous monitoring with AI-powered systems like GOFLOW will be essential for tracking these changes and improving our long-term climate projections. This technology represents yet another example of how artificial intelligence is revolutionizing scientific research.

The Debate Over Federal Education Policy

📝 核心词汇
dismantle /dɪsˈmæntl/ v. dismantle拆除，解散
bureaucracy /bjʊˈrɒkrəsi/ n. 官僚机构，官僚体制

initiative /ɪˈnɪʃətɪv/ n. 倡议，主动行动

The future of the U.S. Department of Education has become a central point of political debate. In a recent congressional hearing, Education Secretary Linda McMahon defended the Trump administration's plan to significantly reduce the department's role in American education, arguing that the current federal education system has failed to deliver results despite spending trillions of dollars over nearly half a century.

The department has already undergone dramatic changes. Its workforce has been reduced from approximately 4,200 employees in 2024 to 2,300 in 2026, a reduction of roughly 45 percent. Many of the department's programs have been transferred to other federal agencies. For example, numerous elementary and secondary education programs have been moved to the Department of Labor, while family engagement efforts now fall under the Department of Health and Human Services. Perhaps most significantly, the massive federal student loan portfolio is being transitioned to the Treasury Department.

These changes have generated intense controversy. Democratic lawmakers argue that the dismantling of the Education Department threatens civil rights protections for students. The department has historically played a crucial role in enforcing laws that protect students with disabilities, ensure equal access to education regardless of race or gender, and hold schools accountable for student performance. Critics worry that without centralized federal oversight, these protections could weaken.

Republicans, however, have largely supported McMahon's efforts. They argue that education policy should be controlled by states and local communities rather than by Washington bureaucrats. One Republican representative told McMahon that he hoped she would be the "last secretary of education," reflecting a long-standing conservative goal of eliminating the department entirely.

Ironically, the situation has also produced some unexpected complications. Despite the overall reduction in staff, the Office of Federal Student Aid is currently trying to hire 334 new employees. Internal documents suggest that the previous staff cuts severely damaged the office's ability to process student loan applications and manage the trillion-dollar loan portfolio effectively.

Meanwhile, the future of special education oversight remains uncertain. The department's responsibilities under the Individuals with Disabilities Education Act have not yet been transferred to another agency, largely due to strong opposition from disability-rights advocates. McMahon has met with numerous disability groups to discuss their concerns, but no clear resolution has emerged. The debate over the federal role in education is far from over, and its outcome will affect millions of American students and families.

近年来，人工智能技术取得了突破性进展，深刻改变了我们的生活和工作方式。从医疗诊断到自动驾驶，从语言翻译到个性化推荐，AI的应用几乎渗透到了每一个行业。然而，技术的快速发展也带来了新的挑战，包括数据隐私、就业结构变化以及伦理问题。如何在推动技术创新的同时，确保技术发展服务于人类社会的整体利益，已经成为各国政府和社会各界共同关注的重要议题。
📝 参考译文
In recent years, artificial intelligence technology has achieved groundbreaking progress, profoundly changing the way we live and work. From medical diagnosis to autonomous driving, from language translation to personalized recommendations, AI applications have penetrated almost every industry. However, the rapid development of technology has also brought new challenges, including data privacy, changes in employment structure, and ethical issues. How to ensure that technological development serves the overall interests of human society while promoting technological innovation has become an important topic of common concern for governments and all sectors of society.

写作题目

Directions: For this part, you are allowed 30 minutes to write an essay based on the following statement: "Technology is a useful servant but a dangerous master." You should discuss the dual nature of technological advancement, using specific examples to support your argument. You should write at least 150 words but no more than 200 words.
📝 范文
The statement "Technology is a useful servant but a dangerous master" captures the essential paradox of our relationship with innovation. On one hand, technology has undeniably improved human life in countless ways. Medical advances have extended lifespans, digital communication has connected people across continents, and automation has freed workers from dangerous and repetitive tasks. Technology, in this sense, serves humanity admirably. However, when technology is allowed to dictate rather than serve human values, problems emerge. Social media algorithms designed to maximize engagement have been linked to increased anxiety and polarization. Automation threatens entire industries, displacing workers faster than they can be retrained. The collection and analysis of personal data by corporations and governments raise serious privacy concerns. In these cases, technology has become the master, and human well-being plays a secondary role. The solution is not to reject technology, but to develop it thoughtfully. We must implement regulations that prioritize human welfare, invest in education that prepares people for a changing job market, and design systems that enhance rather than diminish human autonomy. Technology should remain our servant, not become our master.
