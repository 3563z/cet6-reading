📝 听力原文

The National Aeronautics and Space Administration is testing a revolutionary new computer processor that could transform the way spacecraft operate in deep space. Unlike traditional space computers, which are decades behind modern consumer technology in terms of performance, this new radiation-hardened chip is designed to withstand the extreme conditions of space while delivering computing power hundreds of times greater than current systems. The secret lies in a special error-correction mechanism that can detect and fix problems caused by cosmic radiation in real time, allowing the processor to run at high speeds without crashing. Currently, space missions rely heavily on constant communication with Earth for decision making. However, as spacecraft travel farther into the solar system, the delay in communication becomes a critical problem. For a mission to Mars, for example, a signal can take up to twenty minutes to reach Earth, and another twenty minutes for a response to return. This means that if a spacecraft encounters an unexpected obstacle or needs to make a quick decision, it must either wait for instructions or rely on pre-programmed responses. The new chip would change this by enabling spacecraft to analyze situations and make decisions independently, vastly improving their ability to respond to unexpected challenges in real time.
