The Fusion Energy Race: From Scientific Breakthrough to Commercial Reality

For decades, nuclear fusion has been the elusive holy grail of clean energy — a technology that promises virtually limitless power with no carbon emissions and minimal radioactive waste. Unlike nuclear fission, which splits atoms and produces long-lived radioactive byproducts, fusion replicates the process that powers the sun: fusing hydrogen atoms into helium at extreme temperatures and pressures. The fuel — deuterium and tritium — is abundant and widely available from seawater and lithium.

The past three years have witnessed an unprecedented acceleration in fusion research. In December 2022, the US National Ignition Facility achieved the first-ever net energy gain from a fusion reaction — a milestone that scientists had been chasing for over half a century. Private investment in fusion has surged past $6 billion, with companies racing to demonstrate commercial-scale reactors before the decade's end.

However, significant challenges remain. The two dominant approaches — magnetic confinement (tokamaks and stellarators) and inertial confinement (laser-driven) — each face distinct engineering hurdles. Tokamaks use powerful magnetic fields to contain superheated plasma at temperatures exceeding 150 million degrees Celsius. China's EAST tokamak has sustained plasma for over 17 minutes — a world record. Many experts now believe fusion power could begin contributing to the electrical grid within the next decade.

第 1 题
What is the main advantage of nuclear fusion over nuclear fission?
A. It can be achieved at room temperature
B. It produces minimal radioactive waste and no carbon emissions
C. It does not require any fuel

✅ 正确答案：B。核聚变（fusion）几乎无碳排放且放射性废物极少，而核裂变（fission）则产生长寿命放射性副产品。第一段第一句明确说明。

The Four-Day Work Week Revolution: Redefining Productivity

The COVID-19 pandemic fundamentally disrupted the traditional work structure. Among the most radical ideas to emerge is the four-day work week. The core proposition: maintain full pay while reducing hours, on the theory that employees will be more productive in less time.

A landmark UK trial involving 61 companies and 2,900 employees was an "overwhelming success." Revenue remained stable while 71% of participants reported reduced burnout, 39% less stress. Productivity gains came from structural changes: shorter meetings, fewer interruptions, more deep work. Belgium became the first European country to grant the right to request a four-day week in 2023.

Critics note that continuous-operation industries (healthcare, manufacturing) face challenges. Some question whether gains are sustainable long-term. Nevertheless, the four-day week has moved from fringe to mainstream.
