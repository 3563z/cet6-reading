📝 听力原文

Good morning. Today I want to discuss a growing concern in public health: the impact of ultra-processed foods on cardiovascular health. A major new European cardiology report has recently warned that people who consume the highest amounts of ultra-processed foods face significantly higher risks of heart disease and stroke.

What exactly are ultra-processed foods? These are industrial formulations that contain ingredients you wouldn't typically find in a home kitchen—things like hydrogenated oils, high-fructose corn syrup, artificial flavors, and preservatives. Think packaged snacks, sugary drinks, instant noodles, and frozen meals.

Now, you might assume the main problem is simply too much sugar or fat. However, the study reveals something more disturbing. Even when researchers adjusted for calorie intake and body weight, the elevated risk persisted. This suggests that the processing itself—not just the nutritional content—may be causing harm.

In fact, the report analyzed data from over 300,000 participants across nine European countries. It found that for every 10% increase in ultra-processed food consumption, the risk of cardiovascular disease rose by 12%. What's more, the association was particularly strong for women.

So what makes these foods so damaging? One theory involves the chemical additives used during manufacturing. Many ultra-processed foods contain substances that can trigger inflammation in the body. Chronic inflammation, as many of you know, is a known contributor to heart disease. Moreover, the physical structure of these foods is altered during processing, which may affect how our bodies digest and absorb nutrients.

Therefore, the takeaway message is clear: reducing our reliance on ultra-processed foods could be one of the most effective strategies for protecting heart health. Choosing whole foods—fresh vegetables, fruits, lean proteins, and whole grains—remains the safest approach. As the old saying goes, if it comes in a package with a long list of unpronounceable ingredients, think twice before putting it in your shopping cart.

Octopus-Inspired Smart Hydrogel Can Change Shape on Command

📝 核心词汇
hydrogel /ˈhaɪdrədʒel/ 水凝胶（一种吸水聚合物网络）
breakthrough /ˈbreɪkθruː/ 突破性进展
stimuli /ˈstɪmjʊlaɪ/ 刺激因素（stimulus的复数）
reversible /rɪˈvɜːsəbl/ 可逆的
configuration /kənˌfɪɡəˈreɪʃn/ 配置；结构形态
prototype /ˈprəʊtətaɪp/ 原型；样机
camouflage /ˈkæməflɑːʒ/ 伪装；保护色

Researchers at Penn State University have developed a remarkable new material inspired by the shape-shifting abilities of octopuses. This smart hydrogel can alter its appearance, texture, and shape when triggered by external commands. The breakthrough could have significant implications for soft robotics, wearable technology, and medical devices.

The material works by using a network of polymer chains that respond to specific stimuli. When exposed to certain environmental conditions such as changes in temperature or pH levels, the hydrogel undergoes a controlled transformation. The process is reversible, meaning the material can return to its original state when the stimulus is removed.

What makes this innovation particularly noteworthy is the level of control researchers have achieved. Unlike previous smart materials that could only switch between two states, this hydrogel can be programmed to adopt multiple configurations. The team programmed patterns into the material using a process similar to how data is stored in computer memory.

However, the researchers faced considerable challenges during development. The term "hydrogel" here refers to a network of water-absorbent polymers, not a gel-like substance in the conventional sense. These materials must balance flexibility with structural integrity, which proved difficult to achieve simultaneously. The team eventually solved this by layering different polymer compositions.

A recent study published in the journal Nature Communications demonstrated the hydrogel's potential. The material was able to mimic the texture of natural surfaces such as leaves and rocks, changing its microscopic surface patterns within seconds. Dr. Sarah Chen, the lead author, stated that the current prototype represents only the beginning. "We are now exploring applications in color-changing camouflage and adaptive medical implants," she explained.

Looking ahead, the researchers believe this technology could revolutionize several industries. In soft robotics, it might enable robots to grasp delicate objects without damaging them. In medicine, smart hydrogels could be used to create implants that adapt to the body's changing conditions. As Dr. Chen concluded, "Nature has been our best teacher. By studying creatures like octopuses, we can create materials once thought impossible."

The potential applications extend beyond what was initially imagined. The team is already collaborating with medical researchers to develop smart wound dressings that could change their permeability based on infection levels. Meanwhile, engineers in the robotics field see opportunities for creating more lifelike prosthetic limbs that can subtly adjust their grip strength. The versatility of this hydrogel suggests that its impact may be felt across numerous scientific disciplines for years to come.

Selective Colleges Reach Out to Rural Students

📝 核心词汇
concerted /kənˈsɜːtɪd/ 协同的；一致的；共同努力的
underrepresented /ˌʌndəreprɪˈzentɪd/ 未被充分代表的；代表性不足的
imbalance /ɪmˈbæləns/ 不平衡；失衡
initiative /ɪˈnɪʃətɪv/ 倡议；主动行动
prospective /prəˈspektɪv/ 未来的；预期的；可能的
retention /rɪˈtenʃn/ 保留；保持（率）
counterpart /ˈkaʊntəpɑːt/ 对应的人或物

A growing number of selective colleges across the United States are making concerted efforts to recruit students from rural areas. For decades, students growing up in small towns and farming communities have been significantly underrepresented on elite college campuses. Now, institutions are recognizing this imbalance and taking action.

According to recent data, only about 15 percent of students at the nation's most competitive universities come from rural backgrounds, despite the fact that nearly one in five American children grow up in such communities. The gap stems from multiple factors, including limited access to advanced courses, fewer college counseling resources, and the financial burden of visiting far-away campuses.

Several universities have launched targeted initiatives to address these barriers. For instance, Amherst College recently hosted admitted students and their families from rural areas for campus tours, covering all travel expenses. The term "fly-in program" refers to such initiatives where prospective students are flown to campus at the institution's expense, helping them experience college life firsthand.

However, the challenges extend beyond simple logistics. Rural students often face cultural adjustment when arriving on campus. They may feel out of place among peers from wealthy suburban or urban high schools. Some universities have responded by creating rural student support groups and assigning faculty mentors who understand these students' unique experiences.

A report from the Hechinger Institute suggests that the efforts are beginning to pay off. Applications from rural students to participating colleges have increased by over 30 percent since 2023. What is remarkable is that retention rates for these students are comparable to their urban counterparts, indicating that the support programs are working effectively.

Nevertheless, advocates argue that much more needs to be done. "Access isn't just about getting in the door," noted one researcher. "It's about creating an environment where rural students can truly thrive once they arrive." The long-term goal, they say, should be to ensure that a student's zip code does not determine their educational opportunities.

Critics point out that these programs, while helpful, reach only a small fraction of eligible rural students. They argue that more fundamental changes are needed in how rural schools are funded and supported. Nevertheless, the growing trend among elite institutions signals a broader recognition that talent is distributed equally across all communities, even if opportunity is not. The question remains whether America's top colleges will sustain this commitment as political and economic pressures shift.
