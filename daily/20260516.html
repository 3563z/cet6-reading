Gut Microbiome Particles Linked to Aging

A new study published in Nature Aging has revealed that microscopic particles from the gut may actively drive inflammation and chronic diseases associated with aging. The Mayo Clinic team discovered that tiny particles released by gut bacteria can enter the bloodstream and trigger an immune response that accelerates age-related decline. Remarkably, gut particles from young animals appeared to counter some of these effects when transferred to older ones.

The particles are known as extracellular vesicles — tiny membrane-bound structures that carry proteins and genetic material between cells. The team found that their composition changes dramatically with age. Young mice produced vesicles rich in anti-inflammatory molecules, while older mice produced vesicles that promoted inflammation.

When researchers transplanted vesicles from young mice into older ones, they observed reduced inflammation markers and improved organ function. The findings suggest new therapeutic approaches targeting the gut microbiome could help slow the aging process.

Trump's China Visit Redefines Relations

President Trump returned to the US after a two-day Beijing visit with extensive talks with President Xi Jinping. The summit has been described as a turning point in the complex US-China relationship. Discussions covered trade imbalances, tariff policies, and technology cooperation. Xi raised his observation about the "Thucydides Trap" — the idea that a rising and established power are often drawn into conflict. The timing was significant as both countries face economic pressures: the US with persistent inflation and China with uneven recovery. Whether this rapprochement will lead to lasting change remains to be seen.

Directions: For this part, you are allowed 30 minutes to write a short essay based on the chart below, which shows the number of international students in China from 2010 to 2025. You should describe the trend and give possible reasons.
📊 International Students in China (in thousands)
2010: 265  |  2015: 398  |  2019: 492  |  2022: 350  |  2025: 420

📖 参考范文

The bar chart illustrates the changes in the number of international students in China from 2010 to 2025. Overall, the figure experienced a significant increase followed by a decline and a partial recovery. From 2010 to 2019, the number rose steadily from 265,000 to 492,000, reaching its peak. This growth can be attributed to China's increasing global influence, improved higher education quality, and more scholarship opportunities for foreign students. However, the number dropped to 350,000 in 2022, largely due to the impact of the global pandemic. By 2025, the number recovered to 420,000, indicating a strong rebound. In conclusion, while short-term disruptions affected the trend, China remains an attractive destination for international students, and the number is expected to continue growing in the future.
